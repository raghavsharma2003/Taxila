Kabir practised estimating first and then comparing the exact answer with a nearby rounded number. They correctly calculated 48 × 90 as 4,320 and recognised that it is near 4,000–5,000, but they mixed up “close to” with “the same as” and sometimes rejected an exact answer because it differed from the estimate. This was not fully corrected by the end. At home, ask Kabir to label each result as “estimate” or “exact answer,” then explain why an exact answer can be close to, but not equal to, an estimate.
