## Three laws for every game family

The same three faults appear in all three modes, so they are rules for every family, each held by an assertion in the family's cert (not by a reviewer).

1. **Green means right, and only right.** Green is used for a thing the child got right. The picked row or box takes the focus colour. Her own wrong value takes the mix-up colour. The action button keeps the action colour. (Design's tokens name these roles: answer, focus, mix-up.)
2. **After a miss the screen never holds the key while a try remains.** That covers the key itself, a mark it can be counted off (a triangle on unit ticks), a line it can be traced from (the asked arm), and readings that settle the next pick. The play-out shows what *her* answer means, not what the right one is.
3. **A mix-up is named only by an act a guess rarely produces.** A pick among five or fewer options is reported at chance weight and is not kept on her record from one pick. It is named when it repeats on a second level whose surface differs, or when a constructed act (a typed number, a three-letter name, a placement) agrees with it.

One more, from the first law's cases: **the game never does the skill for her.** If the skill is reading a scale, she produces the number.

## stambh/tally

1. Keep, the "five" state (every total right, rows not bundled): the line points only at "Phir se rakho", and "Jaancho" stays off until something changes. A row gets its green tick only when its marks and its total are both right; a right total over wrong marks gets no tick.
2. Selection outlines take the focus colour (law 1).
3. Read, first miss: run the count over each bundle on the tally, as keep does, so the gap is tied to the bundles. Remove the triangle and the unit ticks from the first-check line (law 2). The hops may stay.
4. A row with no cross drawn is reported as `sign=five` whatever its total. `sign=counted` needs crosses drawn and a total short by exactly the bundles.
5. Kada's icon has one colour in both goals. A digit typed into a full box replaces it (the keys never look off while she is told to change a total). The faded item in the strip is clearly the next one, or is removed.

## kon/place

1. **Measure: she types the number.** After placing, she enters the reading on the number pad. No tap prints a value. The number she types decides the scale (125 against 55), so the band tap can go; keep it only as a mark that prints nothing. Graded within ±2° of the scale whose 0 is on the placed arm.
2. **Draw: after a miss only her arm and "∠O x°" are shown.** The asked arm is never drawn (law 2). The dot gets −1/+1 nudges along the rim (1° is under 2 px on a phone).
3. Size: the question offers all three answers ("∠P, ∠Q, ya barabar?"). A wrong pick does not show both readings while a pick remains. The tick sits clear of both labels. The generator can make any of the three the key (drop "the bigger protractor's angle is never the answer"). Both protractors print the same numbers at a given fade.
4. Evidence: a first-try solve says "chose the right scale" only on levels where the outer scale is the wrong one; the two orientations are served equally. `join-wrong-end` from the two-button choice is at chance weight (law 3), and the two buttons' order varies.
5. A wrong reading's chip takes the mix-up colour. The finished arm carries its B label. The instruction line changes after "Rakh do" to say what to do next. End screens show the number of checks, as tally does.
6. The 11 px placement tolerance stands until it is tried on a phone. The kit's `vertex-off` wording is with Learning (it will be split into two misconceptions); both judges accept showing the game with that open.

## kon/name

1. Find: her wrong pick takes the mix-up colour, never green (law 1).
2. Find: one tap names nothing (law 3). `vertex-name` is named from find only on a second first-letter pick, on a level with different letters, or when it agrees with a `say` miss.
3. Find: the screen must contradict "the same letters are the same angle" without stating the middle-letter rule. Showing her arc's own name is not enough, because to her the two names are one. Propose a form (for example her arc and its name in the mix-up colour set against the asked name as two different things) and put it in the packet.
4. Find: a solve after wrong taps shows a pale tick and the number of checks, as tally does; only the first tap is evidence.
5. Say: one vertex-first name still names the mix-up (a three-tap act and the kit's own sign). No change.
6. Fade 1: the vertex is "O" in the first level only, so "O goes in the middle" cannot pass the fade.
7. Figure: the arcs at the vertex are drawn at different radii with a gap between them; a joining line never crosses a ray; the dashed play-out arc is at least 20 px at 360 and a dashed ray is offset from a solid line under it; O and Q are never served in one figure.
8. "kon" stays (it is the lessons' word for angle).
