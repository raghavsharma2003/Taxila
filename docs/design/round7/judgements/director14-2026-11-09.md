## Decision: dc-r7-script-walk-v6-6 (pages by construction; no model words on a graded turn)

**Reversal clause 1** (more than 5 paging + second-view contradictions with P3, P2′, S7 and S8 in place → one code-drawn board per card, held):
- **The count is 21,** well over 5. S7 and S8 are in place; P3 is in place in three topics, not in c5-t01; P2′ was never triggered.
- **The clause is not taken as written,** for a reason other than its preconditions: its remedy does not touch the mechanism behind 16 of the 21. Those are a close said over the next card's board at a card seam, and a held board per card still has that seam. A held board would also make every picture request a "nothing new drawn" contradiction.
- **What it is right about** is the moving part that keeps breaking. Three runs in a row, a new edit to her line has lost a page, because pages are found by searching the finished text. So that search is removed (P4 below).
- **This is the third run in which clause 1's count was exceeded and not taken.** The director15 clause is therefore unconditional; see below.

**Reversal clause 2** (register below 3.5 with F1, F2 and B1 in place → her spoken framing cut to the authored line plus a one-line code frame; the model writes only the aside reply):
- **Taken.** Register has sat at 2.5–3 for five runs through two remedies (v6.4 and v6.5). F1 removed the repeated frames and register did not move.
- F1 and F2 are partly in place and B1 is not. B1, anchoring model lines to the beat, was the attempt to keep model framing safe, and it failed.
- Every correctness fault the judges trace to the model's words is on a graded or scripted turn: the first false key in nine runs, the invented methods, the doubled whys, the cardless questions, the "break".
- Two judges (c4, c6) name this cut as the fix. One (c5-t02) dissents: the remaining register damage is code's own lines. F1′ answers that.

**Brain:**
- **P4, pages by construction.**
  - `say.js` returns her line as an ordered list of parts: close, closed, bridge, simpler, alt (picture line), aside, body, card. Each part carries its own board.
  - Every later pass (F1, F2, dedupe, Hindi words, the fragment and stray-token drops, any code replacement) edits text within a part and keeps the list.
  - The line is the join of the parts, and the page plan is the list itself. `segmentsAt`'s text search (`spanOf`, `closureEnd`) is deleted from the planning path.
  - Predicates, which throw in tests:
    - every graded close with a next card is ≥ 2 pages, the first on the answered card's board;
    - a picture turn is the alt part on the alt board, then the card part on the card's own board.
- **V1, no model words on a graded turn.**
  - The verdict sentence is a code frame plus the authored why. `bare` model affirmations are never kept.
  - On every scripted turn, her spoken framing is the authored line plus one code frame (clause 2).
  - The model writes only the aside reply: at most 20 words, no lesson numbers, at most one question, and that one about the aside.
  - Acknowledgements are neutral code forms. There are no reads of the child's method.
- **B1′.** A Hindi or clarify request re-says the **live** beat's own simpler from the script's `hi` field. The live beat is the worked step during a worked example, never a closed card. It goes on that beat's board, with the card re-posed after. Code drops any "?" sentence outside the card or the aside.
- **A2.**
  - Pending asides sit in a queue that no later request clears. Each is returned at the first boundary after its promised card, and at the latest at the break or stop. The wrap must check the queue.
  - Each aside gets one park promise.
  - The honest-AI line is said on the turn the child asks, not parked, and once per lesson; a second ask gets a short varied form. It never presumes the child's taste, and it ends on one question to the child ("…tumhe Virat ki kaunsi baat achhi lagti hai?").
  - A return answers what the child said and is never an echo of their sentence.
- **F1′.** Every code frame family gets 3–5 forms and no family repeats within 4 turns, checked anywhere in her line, not just at its opening. That covers the right and wrong verdicts including "&lt;answer&gt; sahi nahi hai", the give-up, the re-pose, the check-ins and "Chalo, aage". Also:
  - A miss and the give-up straight after it never both open on the wrong answer.
  - A try-first gets an invitation ("Haan, pehle tum try karo —"), never "Phir se dekho".
  - Code lines are spoken Hinglish, never code-speak ("1 wala", "card wala sawaal").
  - With V1, model-made stray tokens go.
- **K3.**
  - The last card of a skill gives its key and why on its answer board before the bridge.
  - The parent card is re-posed after its then-card closes, even if a probe intervenes.
  - A second miss is never a bare re-pose.
  - "aage chalo" during a K1 re-pose gives the key and moves on; it never gets the stop menu.
- **Harness H3.**
  - The gate's contradiction counter reads the pages actually drawn (`screen` pages), not `closeBeat.board`.
  - A graded close spoken on a page whose board is not the answered card's counts as a contradiction, and so does a picture turn's card page drawn as the alt view.
  - A fixture of director14's 22 hand-counted rows must be flagged (recall ≥ 20/22) with no more than 2 false flags, before the next paid run.
  - The picture checker's `unrolled` entry gets "patti".
  - The goodbye waits for an open card's close.
