## Decision: dc-r7-script-walk-v6-3 (every line on its own board; turns and promises as predicates)

The teaching layer has now passed six runs in a row:
- 98% of beats taught;
- 100% of keyed answers got a verdict;
- 0 misgrades;
- 0 false keys.

Drawing fell from 44 to 12. What is left is mostly code that can be stated as a rule and checked. So the reversal is not taken. v6.2's T1 and H2 were built as behaviour and did not hold, so in v6.3 each one becomes a predicate that a test can fail.

**Brain:**
- **P1. Every part is drawn on its own source's board.**
  - A verdict or close goes on the answered card's answerBoard, else on its board.
  - A remediation's `say` or `simpler` goes on the remediation's board. Then the then-card gets its own page, which makes the turn two pages.
  - A probe about the answered card goes on that card's board.
  - A line that names a figure goes on the board that draws it. A lone bridge goes on the next beat's board.
  - A page switch never splits one of her sentences.
- **T1′. Predicate: zero turns with two closes, and zero closes for a card not answered in the child's immediately preceding turn.** A close that misses its turn is dropped, never carried. Root-cause why v6.2's T1 let these through, from director11's rows.
- **H2′. Predicates:**
  - The canned aside facts are deleted.
  - A return happens only after a park was said, and only at the promised point, once the promised card is closed. Never mid-card, mid-hint or mid-worked example.
  - The return line reuses at least one content word of the child's own aside.
  - An aside with no return by the end of the lesson is returned to at the close.
- **H1′. Escalation, never repetition.**
  - First miss: the live card's `simpler`. Second miss: the remediation's `say`. Third miss: the answer with its why.
  - No line is said twice for the same card.
  - Only the live card's `simpler`, and only after that card is posed.
- **W1. No answer before the child's turn.**
  - No lead-in that states a yourStep's answer.
  - A worked example poses its problem as a question before "Diya hai…".
- **R1.** Single-page verdicts draw the answerBoard (closes the G3 gap).
- **R2.** Code writes the acknowledgement unless the reply classifier is confident. Hindi templates agree in number ("tukde the"). The raw park template is never spoken.
- **Harness:**
  - Apply the `verdictSeg` fix.
  - The picture check and the `screen` field read what is actually drawn.
  - The c4 child plays every event.

**Studio:**
- **S1. Second views keep the asked mark's position and the scale labels.** A1's key-hiding hides only the key's *value*.
- **S2. Captions use the titles drawn, never internal keys.**
  - shade-grid gets a real second view.
  - The hexagon and other n-gons take unrolled@1 with the shape drawn beside it (e101bdc1).
  - While a second view is up, a line naming the card's original figure brings that figure back.
- **S3.** Board labels switch language with the Hindi mode ("2 equal parts", "slice").

**Learning (data):**
- **D1. A judgement card ("is it equal?", "is X right?") has an answerBoard that draws the judged figure with its answer state**, side by side with the equal version when the why contrasts the two. It never draws only the ideal.
- **D2. Validator:** a why that names a figure must match its answerBoard's template and figure (line, ring, bars, cake …).
- **D3.** Redraw c5-t02 c3 and the s2 worked answer on lines.
- **D4.** Reword c5-t01's "Bhinn hisse ka naam hai, nishaan ka nahi", which names a point as a piece.
- **D5.** Check the source of c4's borderline lines:
  - "Dobara check karke dekha: tukde barabar hain" (unequal laddoo);
  - "upar khaya hua 1 tukda" (3 eaten).
