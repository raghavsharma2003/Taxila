## Decision: dc-r8-director30 (the staged lessons come out of hold three at a time; a step is told only on its cue; a Hindi request gets Hindi; no re-ask is said twice running)

1. **A worked step is told only at a second request or after a wrong answer**, never after a yes, an aside or anything else. The predicate reads told steps as it reads keys.
2. **Every card has a plain-Hindi form of its question** (`hindi`: the question in the Hindi a child uses at home, English kept only where it is the school's word). A request for Hindi at a card gets the easier-words line and that form; the "nothing easier" line is never the answer to a request for Hindi.
3. **An aside reply's turn**: the reply, its joint, then the live part, on every path; never a check-in; where the card is held for a due return the turn ends on the return's question.
4. **The verdict-point-card turn is 65 words**, by the walk: the short reason is said whenever the turn would pass 65 with the full one, and a skill's bridge goes with the verdict (ending on its check-in), the next point with its card.
5. **No re-ask sentence is said in two turns running.** The walk alternates the authored re-ask, the short ask and the card's last question clause; the short ask only after help about the card's own figure (the row left in c5-t02 is closed). A worked example's close is followed by an authored seam into the next card (`joints.afterWorked`) and does not repeat a why said in the turn before.
6. **The scripts' list** (Learning 019), the pilots first: the lines in mechanism 9; a step's easier wording never contains the step's answer (the checker reads key and accept forms); every point changed since the last judged run is read against its board and its card (the checker lists them).
7. **Small:** a picture's caption is chosen by the part it is of; `whyShort` is set where the short reason was said and nowhere else; every row has its `cell`.
8. **The staged lessons, three at a time.** A batch of three is brought to the pilots' standard by Learning (boards part by part, the fit limits with said before fitted, worked examples that ask the deciding step, captions against the view drawn, answer captions, the joints, easier wordings that do not give the step away, the Hindi forms), read by main, run dry, then run live with the four scripted children and judged by the same gate. A script that passes every line with every axis at 3.5 moves from `data/lesson-scripts-staged/` to `data/lesson-scripts/` and is released. One that fails stays staged with its rows. The next three start only after the batch before is judged. The first batch: `c5-maths-ch02-t03` and one script each from class 4 and class 6, chosen by Learning for using board kinds the pilots already exercise.

**What is not changed:** everything `dc-r8-director29` left unchanged, and its items as built; the gate and its strict reading; the stop protocol's check-in; the op cap.

**Work orders:** `docs/patches/to-brain/046-director30-cues-hindi-reasks.md` (items 1 to 5 and 7) and `docs/patches/to-learning/019-director30-pilots-list-and-first-three.md` (items 2, 5's joint, 6 and 8).

**c5-t02 is not re-judged alone.** Its one sentence is rewritten in item 6; the next run, which carries the first staged three, includes the four pilots, and it passes or fails there.

## What would reverse this

- Item 8: a pilot topic that passed here failing a gate line in the next run for a cause in code → the staged batch stops until two runs in a row pass, since the runtime is not yet steady enough to carry new scripts. A staged script failing on script lines only does not stop the next batch.
- Item 2: two judges finding the plain-Hindi form stiff or harder than the Hinglish question → the form is said only at a second request for Hindi, and the first gets the card's re-ask.
- Item 4: two judges finding the short reason at 65 words leaves right answers under-explained → the limit returns to 75 and the bridge rule stays.
- Item 5: two judges finding the rotated re-asks confuse which question is being asked → the authored re-ask is said every time and each card gets a second authored re-ask.
- Item 1: two judges finding a child who says only "haan" at an asked step is left stuck → a yes gets the step's question again once, then the easier wording.
