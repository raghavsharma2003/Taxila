## Main's rulings on the rows the judges left open

2. **A second picture request with nothing left to draw: one contradiction, and a fault in six lessons whatever the count.** The steering child asks for another picture straight after the first (row 16 of every steering lesson; the build added this to walk the path). In three lessons (c5-t01, c5-t02, c6) the hints were spent and **she gave the key**: a child who asks to see something is told the answer and loses the card. The row's record says "hint" there and the README repeated it; both were wrong and three judges corrected them. This path is rebuilt (item 3).
3. **c6's rule is a false key, and it is not new.** "Parimap mein bhujayein jodte hain, guna nahi." ## Decision: dc-r8-director32 (a second lesson goes live; she promises Hindi only where she has it; a picture request never costs the card; the read looks at method and at drawings)

1. **`c6-maths-ch07-t01` goes live** with the two lines of ruling 4, in release 55. The same release corrects c6's rule (ruling 3) and puts the strips' names back in c5-t02's point (ruling 1). Six lessons are then live.
2. **The Hindi switch line is said only where the card has a `hindi` form.** At a card with none, a request for Hindi is a request for help: the card's unsaid help under the ordinary help lead-in, then the card in its other wording, never the re-ask said in the turn before. No line promises Hindi there. Where a card has an honest plainer Hindi sentence it is written (rule 1 of Learning 020 still decides what is honest); the target is every question a child is most likely to stop at, not every card.
3. **A picture request never costs the child the card.** When the card's picture and view are spent, she says one authored line that there is no other drawing of this, gives the card's unsaid hint if one is left, and asks the question again. The key is given on the miss ladder and nowhere else. The row records what it did.
4. **The second help after a miss is the card's own other reason**, on every card: the script marks it wherever the checker's rule 9 finds it safe, and the walk's order is the card's hint, the card's other reason, then the point's other wording. A mix-up met again gets the card's hint before its key.
5. **A question is heard at most two turns running.** After its two forms, the next request gets help with no question on its end and a check-in; at an asked step the cue rule then tells it.
6. **`c4-maths-ch01-t02` stays staged and is rewritten once more** from its report: the roti and the slack-string cards over drawings of their own (item 8 gives the board), no point that states its card's answer, one of the two "Zoya" cards replaced, the own step's question without a negative inside it, and a word for the scissors' arms that the safety reader does not take.
7. **A right verdict before a due return ends on the return's question**, not on a check-in. This reverses my director30 ruling that the return is always a turn of its own; seven judges listed its rows in two runs.
8. **Pictures.** A picture stays up until the question it was asked at has been said. Studio adds a round shape that is not a circle (an oval, an uneven outline) and a slack curve. The bars-as-wholes view beside the same bars, and the tester alone where a turned shape is the question, are replaced by authored pictures of the thing asked about. A picture that lets the answer be counted is redrawn, or the card joins `PICTURE_NONE`.
9. **A script never trips the safety reader.** The checker runs every key, accept form and named wrong answer through the frozen safety predicate and fails the script on a hit. The predicate is read, not changed.
10. **The cold read gains two passes, and the checker lists what they read.** (a) Every sentence that says what an operation or a method is not, or only is, is read against everything the lesson teaches after it; the checker's rule list now includes sentences that end on a bare contrast ("…, guna nahi."). (b) Every board a question is posed over is looked at as a picture: can the answer be counted or seen. The seven scripts get this read again before the next run, by a reader who has not read them.
11. **The script list**, by row in the reports: the asked steps that are recall or a sum (c4 s3, c5-t01 s2, c5-t03 s2 back to its director31 form), the boards of item 7 under "What is left", c5-t02 c1's Hindi form, c6-ch07 s3 e2's point cut to one case, c5-t02's wrap recital.
12. **Carried from to-brain 047 and built this time:** "Dekho," at most three a lesson, the return that uses what the child said, the start line only at the greeting, a reason not said in two turns running, the split of a turn at 66 words or more, the record (`cell.part`, the picture label).
13. **The next staged scripts.** Two new ones are authored under a brief that carries items 2 to 11 and join the next run with c4-ch01-t02, each after the two-pass cold read and mine.

## What would reverse this

- Item 1: `c6-maths-ch07-t01` failing a gate line in the next run for a cause in its script → it returns to staged; a release that carried it is rolled back only if the line is a false key or a misgrade.
- Item 2: two judges hearing a Hindi request at a card with no form as ignored (help given, no word about Hindi) → those cards get an authored line that says the question is already in the plainest words she has, once a lesson, and Hindi forms are authored for every card in that skill.
- Item 3: two judges finding the "no other drawing" line a dead end → the card's hint is authored as a drawing for the cards a child asked at.
- Item 4: a card's other reason said as help that gives its answer away in a judged row → that card's mark is removed and rule 9 is tightened to the pattern that passed it.
- Item 5: two judges finding a child left without the question after help → the question's shorter form is said and the cap is three.
- Item 7: two judges finding the return's question after a verdict abrupt → a one-line joint is authored for that seam; the check-in does not come back.
- Item 9: the checker's safety rule refusing a word a lesson cannot be taught without → the owner is asked; the safety floor is not edited to pass a script.
- Item 10: a script that passes the two-pass read and mine and fails the next run on a rule or a drawing that gives its answer → the staged batches stop until the checker itself can refuse that kind of sentence or board.
- Item 13: a pilot that passes here failing the next run for a cause in code → the staged batches stop until two runs in a row pass.
