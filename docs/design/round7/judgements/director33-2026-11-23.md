## Decision (`dc-r8-director33`, supersedes `dc-r8-director32`)

1. **Release 57 at once: the question that invited "kaatungi" is reworded, and the run is filed.** Nothing else changes in it.
2. **The no-picture turn keeps the screen.** When nothing is left to draw she says the authored line; the board that is up stays up for the whole turn; help follows only if it owns no board or its board is the one already up; then the question, by the cap's rules. A view that would draw the board already on screen counts as spent. *Reverses if* two judges hear the line and the bare question as a refusal of a stuck child; then each card that has no second picture gets one authored, not a help board.
3. **The own reason is said only after its named wrong answer, with no stand-in.** The checker refuses `whyAltAsHelp` on a card that names no wrong answer; the scripts name the answer each reason was written for or lose the mark; every other child gets the hint, the point's other wording, then the key. *Reverses if* cards with a real second reason and no single typical wrong answer are found to leave children with a re-teach that misses; then reasons are written per wrong answer.
4. **Every sentence said after an answer is said over a board that shows it.** An asked step that is confirmed or told draws its result (the cut line), by an answer board on the step; a card posed over a "?" carries an answer board. c5-t01's two worked examples are the first. *Reverses if* drawing the result gives away the child's own step that follows; then the step's sentence is rewritten to claim only what the posing board shows.
5. **The checker refuses what got through** (clause 42): a card posed over a "?" with no answer board; a confirmation or told line that counts gaps, parts or marks over a board that draws a different number; a sentence that compares two fractions ("jitna nahi", "se kam", "se zyada", "barabar") against their arithmetic; `whyAltAsHelp` without a named wrong answer; a given that changes value between two consecutive turns of one problem; one friend's name on more than one person in a skill. *Reverses, per rule,* if it raises more than one false error in three on the nine scripts; that rule becomes a printed line for the reader.
6. **The question cap counts what the child hears.** A turn that ends on the question in any form counts: the card, its re-ask, its Hindi form, the short ask, a picture turn, an aside reply that leads back to it. The invitation is never said straight after a park line or an aside reply, and never in a turn with no help in it. *Reverses if* the lessons then end too many turns on the invitation for a judge's ear (more than four in a lesson named as a fixed form); then the fourth turn at a question gives the key's hint and moves on.
7. **A Hindi request where she has no Hindi is acknowledged truthfully.** One authored line that promises only easier words (`joints.easierWords`: "Aasaan shabdon mein kehti hoon."), then the help. No sentence names Hindi. *Reverses if* a judge hears it as the broken promise of director32 again; then the line goes and the request is met with the Hindi form authored for that card.
8. **The return never asks for what the aside already said**, in code's forms as in the script's (the "when" of an aside that said "kal"; the liking a child already gave).
9. **Learning 022, by row:** the four lines of c4-ch01-t02, then live; c6-ch07-t01's remediation and its s2 e2 board; c4's group rule; c5-t03's own-step "8" and its s3 c3 reason; c5-t02's short reason at s1 e1 and its remediations cut to share a turn with their card; the two new scripts rewritten on rulings 5 and 6 and their judges' rows (the two Golu scales, the "30 centimetre ka scale", the Hindi form that drops "kam se kam", the s2 worked example that asks sums).
