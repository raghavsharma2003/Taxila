## Main's rulings on the rows the judges left open

1. **"These pieces have no fraction name" is a stated wrong rule, and the gate counts a stated wrong rule as a false key.** c4's remediation for unequal parts says "Yeh rassi ek lambe aur do chhote tukdon mein kati, isliye in tukdon ka fraction naam nahi hota" (stuck 58, steering 44), and the wrap says "sirf barabar tukdon ka fraction naam hota hai" (steady 106). Two turns after the first, the lesson's own key names the big piece of an unevenly broken laddoo "1/2" (steering 48 to 50). The judge counted untrue wording and said a stricter reading fails the line. The strict reading is the gate's. c4 fails on it. The same class of sentence in c6-ch07-t01: "1/3 tabhi kehte hain jab teeno hisse barabar hon" (stuck 32, steering 18), against that card's own accepted answer "nahi, sirf 8 cm wala" and its why, "24 cm ka 1/3 8 cm hota". What is true, and what both lessons mean: a piece cannot be named by counting pieces unless the pieces are equal. **The c4 sentences were written by Learning 018 and were said in director30 too (stuck and steering); that judge and main both missed them. Director30's count of three passing pilots was one too many by this reading. In director30 main ruled a badly put reason ("nahi jaate") not a false key; that sentence's rule was true. These are not.**
2. **A picture request answered with nothing new drawn is a contradiction, and a design does not exempt itself.** Before the run the prompt told the judges that over a board with no second view (the angle, shape, measured and word-table boards) she says the board's own caption, "by design: say whether that helps". Two judges said it does not, and both pointed at the gate line. The line stands: c4-ch01-t02's four picture turns and c6-ch07-t01's two (stuck 18, offtopic 30: "drawing karke samjhao" answered by reading a word table back) count. All 20 picture turns heard in the four pilots and c5-t03 landed on boards with a second view and drew something new and the board named. **Corrected the same day:** that is the turns heard, not the scripts. The checker rule built from this decision lists cards in the live lessons whose boards have no second view either (c5-t01 fifteen, c4 seven, c6 seven, c5-t02 two, c5-t03 two); a child asking for a picture there would get the same nothing. The scripted children ask at fixed rows and did not reach them. Item 5 covers them.
3. **c5-t01 offtopic 24 is not a contradiction.** A follow-up card's easier wording and re-ask say "Line ke neeche wale gap socho…" and "Ribbon line ke neeche 12 barabar gaps mein ho, toh…" over a board with one line. Both ask the child to imagine, which the gate allows. Both are faults all the same (the wording states the rule and leaves only 12 − 1; "line ke neeche" names nothing she can see) and are on the script list.
4. **c5-t02's promises at 3 stands.** Four requests for Hindi (steady 36, stuck 8, offtopic 34, steering 8) got "Achha, isko aur aasaan karke bolti hoon" or "doosre shabdon mein samjhaati hoon" and then the card in nearly the same words. Four other judges heard the same in their topics and held promises at 3.5 or 4; one says another such row would put it under. The forms were written this round by Learning 019 and read by main against "is it the same question", not "is it plainer". That read was wrong.
5. **c4-ch01-t02's false returns are a code fault that only this script reaches.** She says "Ab pehle wale sawaal ko phir se dekhte hain" before a card never asked, six times (steady 34, 72, 102; stuck 48; offtopic 30; steering 32). `say.js` takes a card as said before when its last sentence was said earlier, and four of this script's cards end "Kya yeh sahi hai?". No pilot row does it (the judge scanned all 28 lessons). It was in main's dry read of that lesson (two rows) and main did not see it.

## Decision: dc-r8-director31 (the first staged lesson goes live; a rule is true or it is not said; a picture always draws something new; a Hindi form is plainer or it is not written)

1. **A Hindi form is a different, plainer sentence.** Each card's `hindi` is rewritten in the Hindi a child uses at home, in another sentence shape than the card; the school's word stays ("right angle", never "samkon"). The checker fails a form that shares most of its words with its card. The switch line promises only what follows ("Theek hai, Hindi mein poochhti hoon."), never easier words. Where a script has no honest plainer form for a card, it writes none and the request gets the card's unsaid help.
2. **At an asked step a yes gets the step's question once more, then the easier wording at once** (clause 22 as written). The question is never heard three turns running: each asked step gets an authored `reask`, and the walk alternates as it does for cards. The closing question gets a `reask` too.
3. **The short ask is said only straight after the card's own hint or easier wording, in a turn that follows one ending on that card's question.** Never after another wording of the part, a picture, "go on, you try", or on a card whose question asks for an imagined change. Decided by the kind of help, not by comparing figures. Everywhere else: the re-ask or the whole card. **"Back to the earlier question" is said by the card's identity**, never by its last sentence; the checker fails two cards of a script that end on the same sentence.
4. **The verdict, point and card turn fits 65 words in the script.** The checker counts the short reason, the point and the card; a pair over the limit is cut in the point (one example, not two), or listed whole, and a listed one is said as verdict and reason ending on a check-in, then point and card. The first teaching turn after the start line is held to 75 the same way.
5. **A picture request always draws something new.** Every board kind a card uses has a second view, or the card authors another drawing of the same thing for that request; the checker fails a card with neither. Studio 014: a to-scale bar view for a word table whose items are lengths or counts; a second view for the angle and shape boards. Lengths are drawn as bars, not written in tables. **A reason is not a request**: a child's line about how she thought is not read as "easier" at an asked step, help never comes from another part, and no check-in is said inside a worked example on any path.
6. **Help answers the mistake.** After a second miss on a card the next rung is the card's own second reason, before the point's other wording. A give-up at a bridge's check-in goes on to the next point; the bridge is not said again.
7. **Every script is read cold by an independent reader before a paid run**, as clauses 10 and 14 require, and not by its author or by main alone. The reader checks what main's read missed twice: every stated rule as mathematics against the script's own keys and accepted answers; every hint, easier wording and re-ask against its key; for every card, whether its words state its answer and whether its drawing settles it; question wordings that repeat. Main still reads every changed part against its board, and now reads the dry lessons for joints said in the wrong place.
8. **The two staged scripts that failed are rewritten and heard again.** `c4-maths-ch01-t02`: skill 2's cards make the child judge the opening from the drawing (the words do not say inside or outside); the s1 and s2 worked examples ask a step the child decides; the R close's reason is true; each card has its own question wording; the circle cards are not asked over a perfect circle. `c6-maths-ch07-t01`: lengths as bars; the "1/3 tabhi" rule; the two easier wordings and two hints that give the answer; the s2 step says its result. The pilots' lines in mechanism 13 and the wrap's acknowledgements (none that promises to remember, each able to follow anything a child says) are rewritten in the same order.
9. **Small:** told worked steps are said as sentences, not as the script's notes joined with a colon; a why is not said in two turns running; "Dekho," at most three times a lesson; `cell.part` on every row and `noFit` where a turn was split for the board cap; the return to an aside asks something the child has not already told her.
10. **`c5-maths-ch02-t03` goes live as judged**: it moves from `data/lesson-scripts-staged/` to `data/lesson-scripts/`, joins the pilots in the checker and the dry matrix, and is released. Its named lines are fixed with the others and heard in the next run as a pilot.
11. **The next three staged scripts** are written in the next round under a brief that carries items 1 to 8, and join the next run only if the independent reader and main's read pass them before it. The run after this decision carries the five live lessons and the two rewritten staged ones.

**What is not changed:** the gate and its strict reading; everything `dc-r8-director30` built that the judges found working (the step's cue, the reply's seam, the worked close's seam, captions by part, the return waiting for a follow-up card, re-asks as whole questions); the stop protocol's check-in; the op cap.
