## Decision: dc-r8-director20 (authored replies, every promise kept, the gate reads what was said)

**Clauses applied, as set before the run:**
1. **Her J2 reply sentence, more than 1 in 4 odd, flat or wrong:** 3 of 7. **Triggered.** The model stops writing it. The reply moves to an authored, judged library chosen by the aside's topic (R1 below). This is `dc-r8-selection-before-generation` applied to the last sentence the model still wrote in a scripted lesson.
2. **Second clarifies:** 0 in the run. Not exercised; the clause stays open for the next run, which will exercise it.
3. **Listening below 3.5** (c4, c5-t01, c5-t02): triggered. The failing rows are named per request and place, and each becomes a cell (L1 to L4 below).
4. **Register below 3.5** (c5-t01): triggered. The repeated sentences are listed; the largest by far is the card re-said whole (S1 below).
5. **Misgrades, unsaid boards, contradictions:** 0. Not triggered.

**G3, the gate reads what was said (Brain, first).** The dry predicates run over the rows of every live run as well, as a free check printed with the run, and a live run with any predicate above zero is not sent to judges. Every cell whose content is authored (a key and its why, a point, a simpler, a remediation, a joint) is said as written, by code, and does not pass through the reply model. A reply-layer repair may never replace one unit part with another. Fault 5 is the test: its predicate must fail on director20's rows (c4 stuck 12, c5-t01 stuck 12, c5-t02 steering 30) before the fix and pass after.

**R1, the reply after an aside is authored (Learning writes, Brain selects).** Per kind of aside (a sport, a show or film, food, a friend or family, a game, anything else): three return questions that any child can answer in a few words, and three reply forms with one slot for a word of the child's (chosen by the word picker already built), plus forms with no slot for when no word is usable. No form states a fact, a taste of hers, or a promise. A reader who did not write them judges every form against five sample answers before it is used. The model-written path and its predicate are removed from lessons.

**P1, every promise is kept (Brain).** A park is queued whatever else its turn says, and the dry matrix holds: every park has a return before the goodbye; a return is a turn of its own; a park's turn goes on into the live part. If the lesson must end with a park still open, her goodbye names it. After the AI line she does not ask the child a question unless the next turn answers the child's reply (the same library as R1); the predicate is "a question of hers that her next turn ignores".

**L1 to L4, the missing cells (Brain, in the compile's table).**
- L1: a give-up at a check-in gets the point's simpler and then the card; it is never read as yes and never moves to new material.
- L2: a clarify after a key gets the why in its simpler wording, then the check-in; it does not move on.
- L3: a clarify or "pata nahi" at the greeting gets one authored line that says what today is about in plainer words, then the hook.
- L4: a Hindi request on an open card or check-in says that part's simpler in Hindi. Where the Hindi form is the same words as what she just said, she does not promise a change she cannot make: she says the simpler.

**S1, how a card is re-asked (Brain renders, Learning supplies).** After a hint on a card said in her last two turns, she asks only the card's question (its last sentence, which Learning marks as `ask` where the last sentence is not the question). When she comes back to a card from earlier, one authored lead-in joint (`backTo`, three forms) and then the card. "Ab isi par ek sawaal." is said only when the card follows in the same turn. A close that ends a turn is followed by the next part or a check-in, never by nothing.

**W1, no word swapping inside authored sentences (Brain removes, Learning fixes at source).** The English-Hindi twin rule is met in the data: one term per idea per language form in each script, checked by Learning's own gate. Code does not substitute words in a sentence a person wrote.

**Also:**
- c4's worked example must say its final answer after a right answer (fault 9); the predicate exists and c4 slipped it, so the predicate is corrected first.
- A key is followed by a check-in, not by the next card, after a miss as well as after a request (fault 6's remainder).
- Studio: a picture line never contains the open card's key and never has a stripped slot; both become predicates on the picture turn.
- Learning: the three data faults in row 11.
- Harness (Brain): the steady lesson of each topic runs to the end of the script so the third skill is heard; the child answers her return question with an answer that fits the question; the child never answers a card that was not said; the stuck child asks to clarify a second time on the same part.

**What stays as it is.** The held board, pictures, grading, the joints' three forms, the aside park, the compile's order (Loop's asks, then shadow mode). The compile is where L1 to L4 and S1 land: they are cells, not new branches.

**Scale gate after director20:** not met (three topics below 3.5 on listening; c5-t01 also on register and promises; c5-t02 on promises). c6 meets it. The staged 30 stay held from children.

**Reverse if:**
- R1: a judged library reads worse than the model's sentences in the next run (judges prefer the director20 replies): keep the library's questions and return the reply to the model with the library as examples.
- G3: saying authored cells by code makes her sound stitched together (register falls below director20's in two or more topics): the reply model may join parts, under a predicate that every authored part is present word for word.
- S1: a child or judge finds the question-only re-ask unclear without its givens: re-say the givens after the second miss.
