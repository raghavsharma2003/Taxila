## What fails now, by mechanism

Cause, found in code: the rule that a picture line must not state a later card's key (Studio, merged in integration 54) withheld the whole view when neither caption shape was clean, which in a fraction lesson is nearly always. The dry run before the paid run showed the same 10 rows. No predicate counted views. **Fixed today:** the view is always drawn, with a line that names nothing in it when no caption is clean; a gated predicate `pictureDrawn` reads 10 on this run's rows and 0 on a dry run of the fix (16 of 16 views).

## Decision: dc-r8-director22 (what she reads and where she joins are decided once, and counted)

Director21's decision made the order of a lesson a set of laws checked on every row, and this run shows those laws hold. The faults that remain are in the two places still decided case by case (how the child's line is read, and where a joint goes), and in one change that altered what is drawn with nothing counting it.

**P, pictures (done, integration 58).** A picture request always draws its view. A caption that cannot be said clean becomes a line that names nothing. `pictureDrawn` is gated over dry and live rows. For Brain: when a board has no second view at all, she says the part's second wording over the board that is up; the lines that tell the child a picture cannot be made are removed.

**G4, the counts table (process; mine).** The dry matrix prints a table of counts that describe what a child sees and hears: views drawn per picture request, joints per seam by kind, reader agreement, boards changed, cards re-said. Every integration diffs it against the last. A count that moves without being named in the merge stops the integration. This would have stopped integration 54 (16 views to 6).

**R, the reader is measured (Brain; first).** The rule-based child knows what each of its lines is meant as, and writes that on the row. A gated predicate `readerAgrees` compares it with the walk's column on every dry and live row. Then the fix, in one place: what a child's line can be is decided by what she last asked. After her return question or her AI-line question, the next line and only the next line is that reply. After a key's check-in, "pata nahi" and "samajh nahi aaya" are a give-up and a clarify, never a misconception, and "haan" is a yes. While the teach-back is open, a line is its answer or a non-answer and nothing else.

**T, the ending (Brain, Learning).** (1) The teach-back's answer board is up from the first character of the turn that says its key; the predicate reads pages, not the turn's unit. (2) Learning writes three lead-in forms for the teach-back. (3) The wrap is a turn of its own, after the verdict turn. (4) Clause 2 as written: between the authored sentences and the goodbye the model writes one sentence about what this child did, of at most 18 words, naming only cards and values that are in the lesson's rows, with no praise of the child's traits; if the check fails the sentence is left out. (5) Learning writes the "covered" sentence per skill, and the wrap says those of the skills the lesson reached.

**J2, one seam pass (Brain).** No code path adds a joint. After a turn's spans are assembled, one function walks each pair of neighbours and puts exactly one joint at each seam, chosen by the pair of kinds. `backTo` is said when, and only when, the next span returns to a card that was posed before another unit came between. A miss verdict and its remediation always have their joint. Nothing is put between a unit's own point and its card. The rotation is seeded by the lesson, so two lessons do not use the same form in the same place. Predicates: one joint per seam, none inside a unit, `backTo` in both directions.

**H, B, W, K, A (Brain).** A Hindi request gets the part's second wording, or its simpler if that is unspent, never words from her last two turns (predicate). A bridge changes no board: a unit's first board goes up with its first part. After "main khud karti hoon" she says a short go-ahead and waits; a card said in her last two turns is not said again unless the child asks (`repeatedAuthored` stops exempting cards). A key is said once; the why's second wording does not re-say it; a yes-or-no key is said as a sentence. Her reply after an AI-line question uses the phrase forms; her return question uses the script's own `asideReturn` forms.

**Learning (clause 8).** An independent read of the four pilots by readers who did not write them, then: the three untrue sentences and three giveaways; one word per idea inside each script (half a perimeter; bar and patti; hisse and gap); the repeated question frames; c6's cost with the gate asked; second wordings for remediations; the teach-back lead-ins; the per-skill covered sentences.

**Harness (Brain).** The steady child answers the teach-back right in two topics and misses it in two. The false-key flag stops firing on the correct key. The child's intent is written on each row.

**The compile.** Step 3 continues column by column after R and J2, which change the same code.

**What stays as it is.** The order laws. Re-asks. Second wordings. The park replies and the reply library. Every park kept. Grading against verified keys.

**Scale gate after director22:** not met by any topic. The staged 30 stay held from children. Production carries the picture fault in release 27 until release 28.
