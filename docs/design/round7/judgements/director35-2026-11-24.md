## Decision (`dc-r8-director35`, supersedes `dc-r8-director34`)

1. **A round's build is judged before it is released.** The paid run and its judges read the release candidate; it goes to production when the live lessons pass, or with the failing change taken out. Two rounds running, a build that passed every test, CI and the offline walk put a fault in front of children that nine judges found within the hour (release 58: a goodbye stored as a wrong answer; release 60: the two faults of this run). The gate cannot see what a reader sees, and a release costs nothing to hold for two hours. *Reverses if* holding a build keeps a fix for a fault children are hearing out of production for more than a day; then a release that only removes the fault may go first, and anything that changes what she says waits for its run.
2. **Release 61 now: the two faults out.** (a) A hint owns a board only when the script gives it one of its own that is not the board up or the card's posing board; otherwise the no-other-drawing line, then the hint in words, the picture of the turn before left up. The predicate reads the board, not the label. (b) c4-ch01-t02's own step is posed over the turned tester. (c) c4-maths-ch01-t02 returns to staged (clause 53). Release 61 is a fix-only release under item 1's reversal: it restores what release 59 did at these turns and is checked on the offline walk; the next run judges it. *Reverses if* the next run's judges find a picture request answered with nothing new drawn again; then the picture ladder is rebuilt as a list the script states per card, not a rule the walk infers.
3. **A wait says the question (clause 59), in the walk's round (d37).** After the try-first line or a bare "haan", the first wait is the short ask, the second is the card's or step's re-ask, and a third turn with no answer is a turn with no answer: help, and it counts toward the four hearings. A side-topic at an asked step is parked once and does not restart the wait. *Reverses if* two judges hear the re-ask as nagging a child who was thinking; then the second wait is an invitation that names what the question is about, and only the third is the re-ask.
4. **The bridges' easier wordings are rewritten (clause 58):** eighteen sentences, each saying in plain words what was just learnt and what comes next from it, none the bridge again with words taken out; read cold before a run. *Reverses if* the rewritten wording is heard as a second point (a judge counts new content in it); then a give-up at a check-in gets the closed card's reason in its other wording and no bridge sentence.
5. **The four cards get a second picture (clause 54):** c6-ch06-t01 s1's worked step 1, c4-ch06-t01 s1 e3, c6-ch07-t01 s1's first follow-up card, c5-ch02-t03 s1 e2. *Reverses if* an authored second picture settles its card (one judge); then that card keeps the line and the hint in words.
6. **One round changes scripts only, the next the walk only (clause 63), and both are spent on register, one cause at a time (clause 62).** Round d36 changes no line of the walk: it is items 4, 5 and 7 and the register causes that live in the scripts, in the order of the judges' 180 rows: the first teaching turn's length (part of cause (d), 65 rows: the hook or the point's example is cut so that the turn a child hears after "pata nahi" is 60 words with its start line), script sentences and reasons ((b) and (c), 35 rows), and the wordings help draws on ((e), 34). Round d37 changes no script: it is items 3 and 8, the shape of a turn (a verdict, reason, point and card in one breath; a reply and a return run into a new point), "nothing easier" said only when true and followed by the key's reason, and her closes hearing the child ((f), 28: the goodbye's reply, a confirmation that carries the child's own value). Each cause is measured on a run of two topics (eight lessons, two judges, about $0.50) before the next; a cause is done when both judges' rows for it fall by half on the two topics and no other axis falls. *Reverses if* two causes in a row do not move register on their two topics; then register at 3.5 is taken as the level this way of building turns from authored parts reaches, the gate's floor stays where it is, and the rounds go back to lessons and coverage. The split itself *reverses if* a scripts-only round is blocked by a walk fault it cannot work around (a row both judges put in the walk); then that one fault is fixed alone, as release 61 is.
7. **Script rows (Learning 024):** answer boards for the keys of mechanism 7; the reasons of ruling 6 reworded; the sentences of mechanism 12; c6-ch07-t03's s2 step 1 gets its result board. The checker gains three rules: a child's own step has a `board` of its own wherever a step of its worked example has a result board (the author chooses what is up, the walk does not); a named reason's sentence is not shared between two cards of a script; the first teaching turn's 60 words count the start line. The cold read gains a sixth pass: each own step and each follow-up card read against the board it is posed on. *Reverses if* the own-step rule forces a board that gives a step away (as this round's first rule did); then the walk keeps the last result board up through the own step and the script may opt out.

## What I got wrong this round

- **I released before the run, and the run found two faults in what I released.** Both were live from 15:30 UTC until release 61 replaced it the same evening. Item 1.
- **I ordered "the hint owns a board" and never said what owning is.** The builder took it as the board the hint is said over. I read the four `hintBoard` rows of the offline run as the order working and did not look at whether the board was new; the predicate was written from the same definition, so it read 0. Four lessons failed on it.
- **A checker rule I changed made the missing tester legal.** To stop giveaway boards I let an own step with no board of its own carry any answer board; the walk then posed c4-ch01-t02's own step on the worked board while its sentence, rewritten on my order, points at the tester. Five cold-read passes read sentences against sentences and none read an own step against the board it is posed on.
- **The two bridge sentences I wrote for c6-ch07-t03 at the end of the round, unread, are the bridge again.** Its judge heard it. I had recorded that I would not add unread sentences at the end of a round, and then did.
- **The run folder was incomplete** (the harness copies only staged scripts into it), and I did not check it before nine judges were sent to it.
- **The machine restarted while the session sat idle and killed the paid run at two lessons.** It cost fifty minutes and no money; the run script resumes. A paid run is watched in the foreground.
