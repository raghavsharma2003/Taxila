## Decision: dc-r7-held-board (reversal clause 1 taken) with v6.8

**Clause 1** (unconditional; more than 5 paging + second-view contradictions by hand, with the P4′ shape counted → one code-drawn board per card, held): **the count is 8, so it is taken.** It is taken as written, with no ruling to avoid it.

On the same rule, the drawing side has improved sharply (about 25 → 8), and the three mechanisms left are named and fixable. But this clause was set unconditionally because three earlier runs found a reason not to take it, and the reason this time would be the same.

Two of the three mechanisms are also exactly what a held board makes impossible:
- **The silent pin.** No card part is spoken, so the board does not change.
- **The Hindi simpler on a closed card's board.** The board is the open card's.

**The held board (Brain and Studio, specified by main):**
- **The board follows the card.** At every moment the screen shows the open card's board. While the close of a just-answered card is spoken, it shows that card's answer-state board.
- **It changes only where her words begin the next card's unit,** at most once per turn.
  - A unit is a card plus the teaching that leads into it: a point and its ask, a worked example and its yourStep, a remediation and its then-card, a bridge and the next skill's first card.
  - The switch comes at the start of the first spoken part of that unit.
  - Code picks each unit's board from its card; there is no line-driven preselect.
- **No card is on screen that she has not said.** A turn whose card part is not spoken does not change the board. A card that was not said is never graded.
- **A picture request draws its second view beside the held board,** split at 360 px, never instead of it. The picture line is built from the drawn view's own content.
- **Hindi, clarify, aside and "sawaal kya hai" turns hold the board.**
- **Predicates, which throw in tests:**
  - ≤ 1 board change per turn;
  - a change only at the start of a spoken part of a new unit;
  - every spoken part's unit is the held board's unit;
  - no graded answer to an unsaid card.
- **H5, the counter, becomes simple:** each spoken part's unit is checked against the held board, on every turn, single-page ones included, reading the take and not an overwritten ref. Fixture: director16's 8 rows.

**What the held board costs, and Learning's part (D11):**
- A point or remediation that needed its own picture now shares its card's board.
- Learning makes each unit's spoken parts true of the unit's card board, either by rewording or by drawing the card's board to carry both. The validator checks each part's figure against its unit's card board.
- Remediations that relied on their own picture are rebuilt around the then-card's board.

**Brain, v6.8 (beside the held board):**
- **A4.** An aside question ends her turn, and the next card is neither posed nor pinned. The next turn hears the child's answer, gives a one-line code acknowledgement (never graded), then says the card aloud.
- **G1.** A sign mismatch is wrong ("-1/7" ≠ "1/7"). Audit the grader against every scripted key (sign, units, equivalent forms) as a unit-test matrix.
- **V1″.**
  - The "?" drop applies to model sentences only, never to authored lines.
  - The hook's model copy is dropped on every path.
- **B1‴.**
  - "Ab se Hindi mein" is followed by the live part's own hi text, in different words, never the same sentence.
  - During a worked step, a Hindi request gets that step's own line.
  - A simpler spent on Hindi counts as spent, so the next miss gives the key.
  - The greet's "pata nahi" never carries into a later beat.
  - A closed card is never reopened.
- **R5.**
  - Check-ins have 3–5 forms.
  - A worked close says its why once.
  - No "Ab agla kadam" after a remediation, and no doubled "Pehla kadam… Pehle…".
  - Number agreement ("jitne tukde").
  - "jawab Gopi ke paas tha" becomes spoken Hinglish.
  - "aage chalo" never gets the stop menu (c5-t02 steady 64, stuck 62).
