## Why fixes verified by tests failed in the run

Row 4 is the plainest case. Brain's v6.10 report says the clarify row was fixed and pinned by a test on director18's own rows, c4 steering 6 and c5-t01 steering 6 among them. In director19 the same two rows fail the same way. The tests replay a recorded lesson up to a row and check the next move; the paid run walks a new lesson, where her own earlier turns differ. Rows 1, 3, 5 and 9 are faults no test looked for at all: nothing checks that a sentence is not said twice in one turn, or that a re-pose frame is on a re-pose.

There is a run that would have caught most of this for nothing. The harness has a dry mode: all 16 lessons, the rule-based child, the model stubbed, no network. Rows 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 are written by code, so a dry run should show them. I checked the two plainest after the judging, on the same tree: the dry matrix took 27 seconds, the doubled park sentence is in 7 of its 16 lessons, the orphan "Uske baare mein…" return is in 11, and "Phir se dekho:" is in all 16 (42 times). The other rows were not checked dry. Nobody had run predicates over it. A dollar and four judges were spent to find a doubled sentence.

## Decision: dc-r7-script-walk-v6-11 (the dry matrix as a gate; responses compiled, not chosen; the return gets its own turn)

**Clauses applied, as set before the run:**
1. **A picture line with no view drawn:** 0. Not triggered. P6 stays.
2. **Listening below 3.5** (c4, c5-t01, c5-t02): triggered. The clause says the failing request handling is authored in the script and code stops choosing. Taken, as C1 below.
3. **Register below 3.5 with authored joints** (all four): triggered. Every remaining code-written sentence is listed, then authored or removed. Taken, as F1 below.
4. **Misgrades, unsaid boards, held cost:** 0. Not triggered.

**G2, the dry matrix is a gate (Brain, first).** The 16 lessons run dry inside `test:all`, and one predicate file throws on each fault this run found. No paid run happens while it is red. The first predicates, each with the director19 row that shows it:
- a sentence said twice in one turn;
- a joint slot with three authored forms where one form is used twice before the others are used once;
- a slot left unfilled, or a child's first-person words said in her mouth;
- a re-pose frame on a card's first posing;
- a turn that asks the child a question and poses a card;
- a turn that holds a key, a new point and a new card;
- a give-up answered without the key when the card's help is spent;
- a clarify at a check-in answered with a card;
- a planned board change that the record does not draw; a board change under a joint;
- a worked example closed without its final answer;
- an English word and its Hindi twin in one turn (from a list Learning owns).
Every later fix adds its predicate here first, sees it fail, then passes it.

**C1, responses are compiled, not chosen (Brain with Learning).** A pure `compileScript(script)` turns each unit into an explicit map: for every part (point, check-in, worked step, card, remediation, then-card) and every request (clarify, Hindi, repeat, picture, try-first, give-up, "aage chalo", an aside, silence) with the help state, exactly one response, built from the script's own authored fields. The validator fails a script whose map has a hole or two answers for one cell. At run time the walk looks the response up; it does not decide. A predicate in the dry matrix checks every turn's response equals the compiled one. This is the inherited law "one compile() for every lane" applied to the lesson.

**J2, the aside return is its own turn (Brain, Learning).**
- The return says one authored question about the aside, by its topic (`{aside}`), and stops. No card in that turn. The next turn is the child's.
- Her answer to the child's reply is one sentence written by the reply model, under a predicate: at most 14 words, no question, no claim of her own taste, and it must use a content word from the child's reply. If the predicate fails, she says a neutral joint. Then the unit's next part.
- `{said}` leaves the authored forms: Learning re-authors the return forms on `{aside}` alone (D14). Repeating a child's sentence back cannot be made grammatical by code.
- Joint forms rotate across the lesson; none is said twice in a turn.

**F1, the code-written sentences (Brain lists, Learning authors).** Brain lists every sentence and frame that code can say (the tables in `server/brain/say.js`), with this run's counts. Each is then one of: authored per topic in the script, with three forms; kept as a short neutral frame of at most three words; or removed. The re-pose frame is said only on a re-pose. The line that says she is an AI and has no favourites keeps its meaning and is not touched in this pass (the safety floor is frozen).

**Smaller, same pass:** the planned switch is drawn or the turn fails (row 7); the board waits for her first unit part (row 8); the worked answer is said (row 9); a picture line never states the open card's key (row 11, Studio); an aside resumes at the unsaid part of the worked example (row 12). Studio: a view for a regular polygon from its one side label, or the script moves that card to a measured board. Learning D14: the data items above.

**Why:** four runs of fixing the walk by hand in one 4,000-line state machine have each fixed their targets and moved or added faults elsewhere, and the tests written with each fix passed while the next paid run failed. The cure is to stop choosing at run time (C1) and to look at whole lessons for free before paying to look at them (G2). The return (J2) is the one place where she must react to words nobody authored, so it is the one place a model writes a sentence, behind a predicate.

**Reverse if, in director20:**
- The dry matrix was green and the paid run still shows a fault from its predicate list → that predicate is wrong; no further paid run until it fails on the paid run's row.
- Listening is below 3.5 in any topic with compiled responses → the fault is in what is authored, not in choosing: the failing cells are re-authored by Learning with the judges' rows beside them, and the runtime is not touched.
- The model-written reply to an aside fails its predicate more than one time in five, or a judge finds one that is wrong about the child's words → that sentence goes back to a neutral joint, and listening to an aside is taken up as its own piece of work.
- Any misgrade, unsaid board or held cost → as before.

**Order.** G2 and the rendering fixes (rows 1, 3, 9 and the stacks) come first and are small: they go to production as release 17 when the dry matrix is green, because production today still has director18's picture fault. C1, J2 and F1 follow as v6.11, and director20 judges them.

**The 30-topic batch stays held from children.** All four judges say no: every remaining fault is in the runtime or the reply layer and would reach every topic. The regenerated batch itself passed its validator and blind solve; it waits on the runtime, not on its content.

## What this run changes about the plan

The count lines have converged: beats, verdicts, misgrades, false keys and unsaid boards have passed in every topic for two runs, and contradictions went 9 → 1. What is left is not accuracy. It is whether she sounds like one person who is listening. That will not be reached by another list of row fixes, and this decision stops making them.
