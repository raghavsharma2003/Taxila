# What should Asha wear? Research for "more friendly and a bit cool" (round 4, option 4)

Date: 2026-10-10. Question from the owner: "maybe instead of saree there can be something else so that she look more
friendly and bit cool (research on this)". The teacher is Asha, an Indian woman in her early-to-mid 30s, teaching
children aged 6 to 15. The parents must trust her.

Evidence tags: **[V]** I read the primary source today. **[S]** secondary (a news report, a retailer guide, a review
that cites a study). **[U]** my inference.

**Honest limit up front.** No study measures what Indian children call a "cool" teacher's clothes. The measured
evidence on teacher dress comes from Western classrooms and lecture halls. The Indian evidence comes from state dress
codes, which say what parents and officials accept, and from fashion and retail guides, which say what urban women
actually wear to work. So this page sets **the space of acceptable outfits** from the Indian sources and **the
friendly-versus-authority trade-off** from the measured ones. The blind judge on Asha's actual images (JUDGE.md here)
then picks inside that space.

---

## 1. What the measured studies say about teacher dress

| study | who | finding | how it applies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phillips & Smith 1992, *The Effect of Teacher Dress on Student Perceptions*, ERIC ED347151 [V, abstract read] | 60 students in grades 4, 7 and 9 (27 boys, 33 girls), Georgia, USA; one female model photographed in casual, moderate and conservative dress; eight traits on a Likert scale | "Casual clothing was perceived by students to convey teacher friendliness, fairness, and interestingness. **Moderate attire conveyed teacher friendliness, organization, interestingness, understanding, and discipline.** Conservative dress elicited perceptions of teacher organization, knowledge, and disciplinary skills." No gender pattern; some grade differences. | This is the only study I found with **our ages** (9 to 15). The **moderate** middle is the only tier that reads both friendly and in charge. A saree in a school reads as the conservative end (§3). One model, photographs, small n: direction, not proof. |
| Oliver & Marder 2021, Times Higher Education [S] | UK university students | Casual dress (jeans, T-shirt, hoodie) was rated "significantly warmer" and "slightly less competent". Formal dress was the reverse. Warmth and competence both fed students' ratings of teaching. | The same trade-off as Phillips & Smith, in adults. It is the usual finding [S]. |
| Shepherd & Yeon 2022, *Knowledge* 2(2):12 (MDPI) [S: abstract via search; the page refused the fetch] | 92 teacher-education students | Students preferred business-casual dress for their instructors and linked it to approachability. | Again the middle tier. |
| Chatelain, reported in THE 2015 [S] | 112 US students | Smarter outfits scored higher on approachability and likeability. | A counter-finding: "smart" is not the opposite of "friendly". Neat matters as much as casual. |
| Lavin et al. 2010, cited in Whitney 2022 (Cal Lutheran) [S] | — | "Teachers that dress more professionally are perceived as more knowledgeable while casually dressed teachers seem friendlier." | Same trade-off. I have not read the original. |
| Marici et al. 2023, reported in PsyPost [S] | 173 Romanian students aged 9 to 14 | Students who saw their teacher as attractive said they would do more homework and listen more. | A warning, not a design goal: for a child-safety product, "attractive" must never be the lever. Neat and warm, never glamorous (the floor, §5). |

**What I take from it:** the friendly-and-in-charge sweet spot is **neat smart-casual**. That is a step down from formal,
not down to casual. Two Indian student studies point the same way, in their own words. Rural Class VIII students in West
Bengal ranked patience, understanding and caring above subject expertise (*Indian Educational Review* 55(2), NCERT, 2017
[S]). A Hyderabad survey put approachability second only to teaching skill [S].

## 2. What Indian teachers actually wear, and what parents and officials accept

| source | what it says |
|---|---|
| Maharashtra School Education Dept GR, reported by Careers360, 17 Mar 2024 [S] | Women teachers "should wear saree or dress (salwar, chudidar, kurta and dupatta)". Teachers are "not allowed to wear t-shirts, jeans or any other shirt having designs, pictures". The dress "should be neat". |
| Himachal Pradesh, 148 government CBSE schools, from 1 Oct 2026, reported by ThePrint / PTI [V] | Women teachers may wear "saree with blazer", "**kurta/kameez with pant**", "plazo or salwar with blazer" or "shirt-trouser with tie and blazer". Colours: light lilac, lilac purple and navy blue. Earlier advisories also allowed pants and shirts (Tribune [S]). |
| Office-wear guides: Aza Fashions, Harper's Bazaar India "work kurta edit" (Sep 2025), The Jaipur Studio [S] | The **straight or A-line cotton kurta** is the default ethnic workwear for urban Indian women. **Solid or subtly printed** cloth reads office-ready. **Block print reads everyday and casual**. Heavy embroidery reads festive. Cotton, mul and linen are the daily fabrics. |
| Teachers' Day styling guides (House of Kari) [S] | A printed cotton kurta set is "the balance between festive and professional" for a classroom. |
| A Bihar district magistrate scolded a head teacher (a man) for a white kurta-pyjama: "looked like a people's representative", Kerala Kaumudi 2022 [S] | **Political coding is real.** White khadi and the sleeveless Nehru ("Modi") jacket read as politician in India. I dropped the khadi Nehru-collar jacket from the brief's list for this reason [U]. |

**What I take from it:**
- A **kurta without a dupatta over trousers** is mainstream and accepted. It is in the 2026 Himachal list as "kurta with
  pant", and it is the office default.
- **Denim is the one place parents could object.** Maharashtra bans jeans. A denim jacket is not jeans, but it is the
  nearest thing to "casual" in the set. So I tested it rather than argued it (JUDGE.md).
- **The saree** is fully accepted, but in a school it sits at the conservative, formal end, the Phillips & Smith
  "conservative" tier: organised, knowledgeable, disciplining, and the least friendly. That fits the owner's instinct.
- **Prints should be sparse and small.** A dense print reads festive or busy, and at a 38 px chip it turns to noise.

## 3. How premium apps and games dress mentor characters

- **Duolingo** (official blog, "female character origin stories" [V]). The cast was built for "different ages,
  ethnicities, and personalities" so every learner relates to someone. Each character is a personality first.
  Secondary analyses [S] note that each character has one primary colour and one signature garment (Lily's dark
  hoodie, Zari's bright pink). **Lesson: one signature silhouette plus one owned colour, the same in every frame.**
- **Ms Frizzle** (*The Magic School Bus*): the best-loved "cool teacher" in children's animation wears an ordinary
  dress made memorable by one personal motif (planets, constellations) [S, WeAreTeachers]. **Lesson: "cool" comes
  from one personal, slightly playful detail on otherwise ordinary clothes, not from fashion.**
- **Prakash's own rules (world SPEC §1, §6):** lamp gold `#FFC24D` is the only hot colour, and it means "your move" and
  "understood". Violet is the shadow colour. **Asha's teal is the listening colour**: `--listen #63D6CB` was lifted
  from her teal. Three constraints follow:
  1. no large lamp-gold or saffron field on her (it would compete with the one hot accent);
  2. no violet or lilac garment (it would merge into the shadow planes; the Himachal lilac is out for this reason);
  3. keep **teal as her owned colour**, so the listening ring stays visibly "hers".
- **The jharokha's lamplight is cream**: an ivory or white garment at the shoulders would vanish into the window's
  light, and she would lose her silhouette at 38 × 48. Garments should be mid-to-dark against the cream.

## 4. Colour and silhouette for approachability

- **Colour:** the colour-and-emotion literature is weak for clothing [S]. Light and warm colours associate with
  positive emotions. Blue and green associate with calm (Kaya & Epps 2004, as summarised [S]). An Oklahoma State press
  release (2026) says orange-wearers scored higher on peer approachability [S], with methods not published. I treat all
  of this as weak. The usable conclusion is narrow: **a calm cool base (teal or indigo) with one warm accent (rust or
  terracotta)** reads calm and warm together, and it fits Prakash's palette.
- **Silhouette** [U, from character-design practice]: soft shoulders and an open, relaxed line read friendlier than
  the saree's strong diagonal pallu and border, which reads formal and ceremonial. A band or shirt collar gives a
  neat, "organised" edge (Phillips & Smith's moderate tier). A layer worn open (a jacket or cardigan) reads relaxed
  and "ready to work".
- **Small real cues** that make a teacher read as a person: small oxidised-silver studs instead of gold (everyday,
  slightly artisan, a touch "cool") and a pen clipped in a pocket. A watch and rolled sleeves are outside the head-and-
  shoulders crop, so they cannot help here.

## 5. The floor (product, not compliance)

These rules come from TEACHER-VISUAL and the ASHA brief. Every candidate must meet them:
- adult;
- modest everyday professional clothing: neckline at or above the collarbone, a straight relaxed cut, never
  figure-hugging;
- no glamour, no make-up beyond natural (the lips were fixed to an unpainted brown-rose, see the age front);
- no romance or flirtatious register;
- nothing a parent would read as unprofessional;
- Indian and specific, but no caste, region or religion marker beyond everyday wear.

On that last rule: the bindi is part of her identity from the chosen face, and it is everyday wear for many urban Indian
women, so it is kept. Ajrakh is a Kutch and Sindh craft with community provenance, so the block print is a generic
sparse **dabu or Bagru-style** small motif, which reads as Fabindia-everyday rather than regional.

Every candidate is an edit of the same age-strengthened front, with the same face, hair and studs. Only the clothes
change, so the comparison isolates the outfit:

**Project history:** the denim option is not new. TEACHER-VISUAL §4.3 (2026-10-03) briefed `asha` as "kurti with a
denim jacket, high ponytail, small studs", at age 24. The saree belonged to the draft third tutor `uma` (34, "handloom
saree, low bun"). Round 4 made Asha grown-up and gave her uma's saree with the face. So o2-denim is partly a return to
her original brief, now at 34.
