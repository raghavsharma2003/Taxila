# Detecting understanding in tutoring dialogue: 2025-2026 papers, mechanisms and measured accuracy

Date: 2026-10-02. Scope: what the 2025-2026 literature (arXiv, EDM, AIED, LAK, L@S, BEA, CHI, ACL/EMNLP, Dev Science)
says about knowing whether a learner understood **without quiz-style testing**, and which mechanisms Taxila's
`server/comprehension/**` can implement. Complements, does not repeat, `docs/research/learning-science.md` §1 and §7
(older literature, probe catalogue P1-P24) and `docs/research/learner/LEARNER-MODEL.md` / `STUDENT-SIM.md`.

**The single most important caveat.** Almost none of this work is on Indian children aged 9-12, in voice, in
Hindi-English. Most samples are undergraduates (programming, algorithms) or UK/US secondary maths in text chat.
Every accuracy number below is an upper bound for Taxila until replicated on our own transcripts [U].

## 1. Headline findings (what changed since learning-science.md was written)

1. **Dialogue knowledge tracing is weak on real students.** LLMKT (Scarlatos, Baker & Lan, LAK 2025) reaches AUC
   **76.7 on MathDial but only 65.8 on CoMTA**; MathDial's students are LLM-simulated, CoMTA's are real. Classic KT
   (BKT, DKT, AKT, SAINT, simpleKT) sits at ~47-53 AUC on CoMTA, i.e. chance. Majority-class baseline: Acc 57.8,
   AUC 50 [V, full text Table 2]. learning-science.md §1.12 quoted "~76% AUC"; that is the **simulated** dataset —
   on real learners the number is ~66. Correct this in `context/measurements.md`.
2. **LLM correctness labelling of a turn is ~76-83% accurate; humans ~86%.** GPT-4o final-turn correctness accuracy
   75.8% on the full set, 83.3% on the human-evaluation subset; human annotators 85.6% [V, Table 4]. So per-turn
   "was this right?" labels from an LLM carry ~15-25% noise before any downstream modelling.
3. **The "correct answer trap" is the dominant failure.** On 20,964 real Eedi responses, hidden misconceptions behind
   correct answers were caught 57% (fine-tuned classifiers) vs 84% (open-weight reasoning model), but at realistic
   prevalence **false alarms outnumber true detections ~8:1** (Imran & Bulathwela 2026, arXiv 2606.23205) [V]. The
   companion AIED'26 paper reports ~4 false alarms per genuine detection for the best model and that 71% of failures
   concentrate in two question types (arXiv 2605.23925) [V]. *Note:* the two abstracts disagree on which model type
   scored 57% vs 84% (one says fine-tuned T5 84% / frontier LLMs 57%); the direction that holds in both is
   "detection is possible, precision is terrible" [V, discrepancy flagged]. Both papers propose **detect → verify with
   a diagnostic follow-up → escalate** rather than acting on detection.
4. **Mid-range answers are where automated scoring breaks.** GPT-5.2, GPT-4o and Claude Opus 4.5 (few-shot) score
   fully-right and fully-wrong biology short answers well but "exhibit substantial degradation on mid-range responses";
   fine-tuned BERT-class encoders do best on the middle; human agreement is flat across levels (Gurin Schleifer et al.
   2026, arXiv 2605.07647) [V]. Partial understanding — exactly the state we most need to detect — is the hardest.
5. **LLM cognitive diagnosis from student work is below F1 0.5.** 18 LLMs on 3,036 teacher verdicts (MathCog,
   TIMSS-grounded checklists): "All models underperform (F1 < 0.5)", worse on vague evidence, with **fabricated
   evidence** as a systematic error (Kim et al., arXiv 2504.00843, rev. 2026) [V].
6. **Multi-agent, behaviour-specific reasoners beat one LLM by 3x for stealth assessment.** In a financial-literacy
   platformer (193 K-12 players, 264 sessions), four domain agents labelling behavioural events into BKT gave mastery
   that correlated with post-test r = 0.333 and learning gain r = 0.276, with **no correlation with pre-test**
   (discriminant validity); a single-LLM baseline gave r = 0.095 (n.s.). Event classifier vs 3 experts: Fleiss κ =
   0.624 (Santos, Julia & Nascimento 2026, arXiv 2606.25358) [V]. This is the closest published analogue to Forge-game
   telemetry → per-concept mastery.
7. **Goal recognition improves concept-level stealth assessment in games.** 119 middle-schoolers in a microbiology
   game; adding predicted in-game goals to gameplay traces + written reflections improved accuracy on all four concepts
   and converged earlier (Gupta et al. 2026, *JRTE* 58(1)) [S, numbers not retrieved].
8. **LLM student simulators abandon misconceptions on any feedback.** Seven LLMs (4B-120B) show near-zero Selective
   Flip Score: they correct equally after relevant and irrelevant feedback; SFT raises SFS by up to +0.56
   (Do, Sonkar & Sachan 2026, arXiv 2605.12748) [V]. Already absorbed in STUDENT-SIM.md SIM1; reconfirmed. Related:
   the "competence paradox" and an Epistemic State Specification for valid simulation (Yuan et al. 2026,
   arXiv 2601.05473) [V]. **Never validate a comprehension detector only against LLM-simulated children** (finding 1
   is the empirical proof: 76.7 simulated vs 65.8 real).
9. **Real learners bypass scaffolding.** 9,490 chats across nine datasets: real-world students show lower uptake and
   "frequently bypass the chatbot's pedagogical framing" (Neagu et al. 2026, arXiv 2606.15766) [V]; 12,650 messages /
   500 conversations: students "mainly use them for answer-extraction" (Kobler et al., ACL 2026, arXiv 2604.23486) [V].
   A covert probe that only fires when the child follows the tutor's script will be starved of data [U].
10. **Teaching a peer agent works for primary children, most for low-prior-knowledge ones.** 58 primary children
    teaching an autonomous robot (French vocab + grammar) vs tablet self-practice: significantly higher retention,
    especially grammar; lower prior knowledge benefited most (Tarakli et al. 2025, arXiv 2506.18365) [V]. AlgoBo
    (CHI 2024): LLM tutee with prescribed misconceptions via a **Reflect-Respond** pipeline; its "why/how" questions
    produced knowledge-dense conversations, effect size 0.71, n = 40 novices [S]. Teach-back is both learning move and
    the richest probe — and it now has child evidence.
11. **Children's disfluency tracks accuracy, not confidence.** 5-8-year-olds: more fillers, hedges, longer onsets on
    *incorrect* trials; fluency tracks accuracy reliably but predicts confidence only when confidence and accuracy
    align (West, Baer, Yu & Odic 2025, *Dev Science* e13617) [S, abstract via search]. So disfluency is a (weak)
    signal about **correctness**, which is useful; it is not a window into what the child thinks they know.
12. **Audio LLMs encode paralinguistics but lose them before the output.** Whisper-large-v2, Qwen2-Audio, Qwen2.5-Omni,
    Chroma: speaking style is strongly encoded in the top third of the audio encoder and "consistently degraded before
    reaching the output" (arXiv 2609.00727) [V]. Asking a realtime voice model "did the child sound unsure?" is
    unreliable; **extract features (latency, fillers, onset, pitch range) in code** from the audio/ASR stream [U].
13. **Webcam gaze is not a usable comprehension signal.** 354 participants, online reading: AUROC 0.60 for broad
    neurodivergence, 0.53-0.61 for specific diagnoses, authors warn against individual use (Jaiyeola et al., EDM 2025)
    [V]. Lab eye-trackers predict children's reading comprehension (ETRA 2025, random forests) [S]; consumer-phone
    webcams in Indian homes will be worse [U]. **Do not build on gaze.**
14. **Scoring practice: reasoning effort helps, ensembling does not.** 900 high-school maths conversations: temperature
    sampling beat deterministic calls, ensembles of 1→7 gave no meaningful gain, reasoning effort had a significant
    positive linear trend with accuracy; small no-reasoning models were best cost/performance (Frohn, NCME 2026,
    arXiv 2604.26954) [V].
15. **BEA 2025 shared task: automatic pedagogical judging tops out at macro-F1 ~0.58-0.72** on mistake
    identification / location / guidance / actionability, against human κ ≈ 0.65; Prometheus-style LLM judges
    correlated poorly or negatively with humans on MRBench (Maurya et al. 2025) [S]. LLM-as-judge is not a grader.

Effectiveness context (not detection, but says "the loop is worth building"):
- Eedi × Google DeepMind LearnLM RCT, 165 UK secondary students: LearnLM-supervised tutoring raised solving a **novel
  problem on a later topic** by +5.5 pp over human tutors; 0.1% of messages had factual errors, zero harmful
  (LearnLM Nov-2025 report) [S]. Their outcome measure *is* a delayed transfer probe — the evidence type we want.
- Medly micro-RCTs, GCSE science, 644 completers: g = 0.33 (95% CI 0.18-0.48); Biology 0.52 (Harrison et al. 2026,
  arXiv 2609.14789) [V].
- PAGE (semester deployment): content personalised to student major/interests gave significantly better outcomes and
  higher perceived relevance and trust (Lim et al. 2025, arXiv 2509.15068) [V, no effect size in abstract].
- Rori (Ghana, WhatsApp, grades 3-9, ~500 students): ~0.3 SD / +11 pp in maths at ~USD 5 per child [S].

## 2. Mechanisms we can implement, with evidence

Each row: what to build, the measured evidence, and the Taxila rule. "M#" ids are for `server/comprehension/**`.

### M1. Two-stage dialogue KT: LLM labels, small model traces (LLMKT / DiffKT pattern)
- **Evidence.** LLMKT: LLM labels each turn's KCs + correctness, a KT model traces mastery; beats all KT baselines but
  real-student AUC 65.8 [V]. Huang, Scarlatos, Lee & Lan 2026 (arXiv 2605.01097) add **per-turn difficulty** and map
  LLM outputs to IRT ability/difficulty, outperforming KT baselines with interpretable parameters [V, numbers not in
  abstract]. Training tutors with an LLM student model as reward (Scarlatos et al., AIED 2025) [V].
- **Rule.** Keep LEARNER-MODEL §6.1: the LLM is a *labeller*, the ledger is BKT/IRT in code. Add **item difficulty per
  probe** (an IRT b-parameter on each kit item/probe) so a correct answer on an easy recall probe moves the belief less
  than on a hard transfer probe [U]. Expect real AUC ~0.65-0.70 from dialogue alone — which is why M2-M7 exist.

### M2. Detect → verify → escalate (never act on one detection)
- **Evidence.** Hidden-misconception detection has 4-8 false alarms per true hit at realistic prevalence [V];
  ECD-based multi-agent CBA (ETS, AIED 2025: expert + peer agents ask leading questions, formative + summative
  assessor agents write evidence statements, a non-LLM **Watcher** controls flow) [S].
- **Rule.** A detected misconception is a **hypothesis** that schedules a discriminating follow-up probe (contrasting
  case, "why", or a planted teacher error that the misconception predicts the child will accept). Only the follow-up's
  verified-key outcome moves the misconception layer. The Watcher = our Director (code), not an LLM [U].

### M3. Generate → retrieve → rerank misconception diagnosis from dialogue
- **Evidence.** Mitton et al. (Eedi, L@S 2026 oral, arXiv 2602.02414): a fine-tuned LLM generates candidate
  misconceptions, embedding retrieval narrows to a closed library, a second fine-tuned LLM reranks; fine-tuned small
  models beat larger closed models; both stages matter in ablation [V]. Personalised distractors from a student's own
  past errors (MCTS reasoning reconstruction, 1,361 students, ACL 2026) [S].
- **Rule.** Diagnose **into a closed, kit-verified misconception library per NCERT outcome** (learning-science §1.13
  moat asset), never free-text. Use the library entry's predicted wrong answers to build M2 follow-ups and Forge game
  traps. Fine-tuning is permitted on Azure OpenAI models (Foundry fine-tune) [U: check model availability].

### M4. Textual knowledge-state summary as an auditable bottleneck (LBM)
- **Evidence.** Language Bottleneck Models: encoder LLM writes the minimal natural-language knowledge state that
  explains past answers and predicts future ones; competitive accuracy, better sample efficiency, can express
  misconceptions (Berthon & van der Schaar, arXiv 2506.16982) [V via search abstract]. History-aware profiles trained
  with RL improve student-response prediction (Duan et al., EMNLP 2026, arXiv 2605.30051) [V].
- **Rule.** Fits TeacherMemory (§6.8): a per-concept "what she said / what she believes" note **rendered as shapes,
  not sentences** (inherited law). Its value must be measured as *predictive*: does adding the summary raise held-out
  AUC on the next probe? If not, drop it [U].

### M5. Game telemetry → domain-specific event agents → BKT
- **Evidence.** Agentic KT in a game: r = 0.333 with post-test, 3x a single LLM, κ = 0.624 event labels [V]; goal
  recognition adds concept-level accuracy (Gupta 2026) [S]; Physics Playground lineage [learning-science §1.4].
- **Rule.** Forge games emit typed events (FACTORY.md telemetry); **code** classifies most events (strategy used,
  error class, which misconception's trap was triggered), an LLM only for open moves (free-text, drawings). One small
  classifier per concept family, not one generalist. Validate with the discriminant check they used: game mastery
  must correlate with a *later* probe, **not** with the pre-lesson placement [U].

### M6. Teach-back to a protégé that holds a scripted misconception (AlgoBo Reflect-Respond, robot tutee)
- **Evidence.** Children 8-ish: higher retention, largest for low prior knowledge [V]; AlgoBo d = 0.71 knowledge
  density [S]; Kobayashi 2024: benefit only if the child *expected* to teach [learning-science §1.5].
- **Rule.** Announce at concept start. The protégé's knowledge state is **code-held** (STUDENT-SIM SIM1: code decides,
  skin words) so it does not sycophantically "get it" on any explanation (finding 8). Score the child's explanation
  by expectation coverage + whether the child corrects the protégé's planted misconception; the latter is a verified
  key. Safety: protégé is a character, not a peer/friend register [U].

### M7. Delayed, interleaved transfer probes (2-3 topics later; next session)
- **Evidence.** The LearnLM RCT's primary outcome is a novel problem on a subsequent topic [S]; forgetting-aware
  mastery (TASA, AAAI-26 workshop, arXiv 2511.15163) gives better outcomes than static mastery [V, no numbers];
  learning-science §1.9: delayed summaries diagnose at G = 0.70 vs 0.29 immediate.
- **Rule.** Mastery claims require a **delayed** success; immediate in-lesson success only sets "provisional". Belief
  decays with a forgetting curve; a probe is scheduled when the decayed belief crosses the band. Delayed probes are
  woven into the next topic's examples (the child meets it as part of new work, not as revision) [U].

### M8. Code-extracted voice features as tie-breakers only
- **Evidence.** Disfluency tracks accuracy in 5-8-year-olds [S]; audio LLMs lose paralinguistics before output [V];
  multimodal (text + prosody + temporal) inference of understanding in a 45-student university pilot, "moderate"
  gains (arXiv 2512.10441) [S]; KidSpeak child-speech LLM 87% average across four tasks (arXiv 2512.05994) [V];
  ChildVox benchmark for child audio (arXiv 2605.29257) [V, no numbers].
- **Rule.** Features computed in code from ASR timestamps + audio: onset latency, filler/hedge count ("umm", "shayad",
  "maybe", "I think"), self-repairs, rising terminal pitch — each **z-scored per child per probe type**, excluded when
  ASR confidence is low or a language switch occurred in the turn. Use as likelihood ratio ≤ 1.5 or as a trigger for an
  M2 follow-up; never alone [U, LR uncalibrated].

### M9. Grader configuration
- **Evidence.** Reasoning effort helps, ensembles don't (Frohn 2026) [V]; mid-range degradation, fine-tuned encoders
  best on partial answers [V]; self-explanation scoring (paraphrase / bridging / elaboration) F1 0.72-0.89 (EDM 2025
  "One Model to Score Them All") [S]; programming self-explanations LLM acc 0.96 vs 0.65 semantic similarity
  (arXiv 2605.21614) [S].
- **Rule.** Grade **against the kit's expectation list and misconception list** (AutoTutor EMT), one rubric item at a
  time, reasoning model at moderate effort, single sample at non-zero temperature. Route "partial" verdicts to a
  follow-up probe instead of scoring them (the band the graders get wrong). Calibrate on a 300-turn human-labelled
  Indian-child set before trusting any threshold [U].

### M10. Bypass-tolerant probing and answer-seeking detection
- **Evidence.** Students bypass scaffolding (findings 9); answer-leakage attack taxonomy for LLM tutors
  (arXiv 2604.18660) [S]; verification feedback had the best productive-continuation rate (82.4%) and direct answers
  the worst (62.7%) in 16,851 StudyChat responses (Abrar et al. 2026, arXiv 2607.09919) [V].
- **Rule.** "Just tell me" is evidence (low independence / possible wheel-spinning), not a failure to route around.
  Prefer *verification* moves ("check mine — is this right?") which are also discriminative probes [U].

### M11. Personalising framing to the child (vibe), measured not assumed
- **Evidence.** Interest-personalised content improved outcomes over a semester (PAGE) [V]; TASA persona + memory
  [V]; learning-styles crossover still absent (learning-science §2).
- **Rule.** Personalise **examples, interests, humour, pace and formality** (affect engagement); personalise
  **representation** only from this child's measured re-teach success (LEARNER-MODEL §6.5 format-efficacy layer).
  Re-teach choice = the representation with the highest posterior "worked after a failure" for *this* child, with
  an exploration floor [U].

## 3. What not to build (and why)

| idea | why not | tag |
|---|---|---|
| Free-form "has the child mastered X?" LLM verdict | F1 < 0.5 on cognitive diagnosis; fabricated evidence | [V] |
| Acting on a single misconception detection | 4-8 false alarms per hit | [V] |
| Asking a speech-to-speech model to judge uncertainty | paralinguistics lost before output | [V] |
| Webcam gaze for comprehension | AUROC ~0.6 in adults with lab software | [V] |
| Validating detectors on LLM-simulated children only | simulated AUC 76.7 vs real 65.8; sycophantic flips | [V] |
| Ensembling graders for accuracy | no gain 1→7 | [V] |
| LLM-as-judge for pedagogical quality gates | poor/negative correlation with humans (MRBench) | [S] |
